Lesson notes: the Factory Acts

The Factory Act of 1833 applied to textile mills. It banned the work of children under 9. Children aged 9 to 13 could work at most 9 hours a day, and young people aged 13 to 18 at most 12 hours a day. Children had to receive two hours of schooling every day. The Act appointed four factory inspectors to enforce it, which was its most important new idea.

The Mines Act of 1842 followed a report on child labour in the coal mines. It banned all women and girls, and boys under 10, from working underground.

The Ten Hours Act of 1847 limited the working day of women and young people in textile mills to 10 hours.

Question for the class: why did Parliament protect children first, and adult men only much later?
